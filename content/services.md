You have raised your round. The board wants a roadmap, your engineers want direction, and the
architecture decisions you make in the next six months will either compound or haunt you. What you
need is executive judgment — not necessarily forty hours a week of it.

That is the gap I fill.

## How I engage

### Fractional CTO

For companies with no technology executive, or a founding engineer who has outgrown the role.

I own the technical strategy end to end: the roadmap, the architecture, the build-versus-buy calls,
the vendor relationships, and the story you tell investors about your technology. I sit in your
leadership meetings and I am accountable for engineering outcomes, not just advice.

**Typical shape:** one to three days a week, three months minimum, reviewed quarterly.

### Fractional VP Engineering

For companies where the technical vision is clear but delivery is not.

This is the execution seat: team structure, hiring plans, performance management, the SDLC, CI/CD,
and the operating cadence that turns a roadmap into shipped software. I have taken a team from one
release per quarter to multiple releases per week by rebuilding exactly these things.

**Typical shape:** two to three days a week, six months minimum.

### Diligence & compliance readiness

For companies raising, selling, or entering a regulated market.

Architecture review, security and compliance posture, engineering team assessment, and the
remediation plan to close the gaps — before someone else finds them. I have led SOC 2 Type II, PCI
DSS, HIPAA, HITRUST, and GDPR programmes, and I have sat on both sides of an acquisition table.

**Typical shape:** a four-to-six week engagement, or ongoing advisory.

## What the first 90 days look like

Every engagement starts the same way, because you cannot fix what you have not seen.

**Days 1–14 — Listen and assess.** I talk to every engineer, your product leadership, and your most
frustrated customer-facing colleague. I read the code, the incidents, the backlog, and the last two
quarters of delivery history. You get a written assessment: what is actually true, what is at risk,
and what I would change first.

**Days 15–45 — Stabilise.** We fix the thing that is bleeding. Usually that is delivery cadence,
an architecture decision that is blocking three other decisions, or a compliance gap with a deadline
attached. This is where you should start feeling the difference.

**Days 46–90 — Build the machine.** Team structure, hiring plan, technical roadmap tied to your
business milestones, and the operating rhythm that keeps it on track after I step back. The goal is
that the improvement survives my departure.

By day 90 you should have a roadmap your board understands, an engineering team that knows what it
is building, and a clear-eyed view of what you need from a permanent CTO — including whether you
need one yet.

## Who this works for

This is a good fit if you are a **Series A or B company in Healthcare Tech or FinTech**, somewhere
between five and fifty engineers, and you have real revenue or real funding behind you.

It is a **poor** fit if you are pre-seed and need someone to write the first version of the product,
or if you want a contractor to execute a plan that is already settled. I am most useful where the
hard part is judgment, not hands.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does a fractional CTO actually do?**
A fractional CTO provides executive-level technology leadership part-time: the technical strategy,
architecture decisions, engineering org design, and hiring plan a full-time CTO would own — at the
fraction of capacity your stage actually needs. For most Series A and B companies that is one to
three days a week.

**How does the cost compare to a full-time hire?**
A full-time CTO at Series A or B typically costs $250,000–$400,000 plus meaningful equity. A
fractional engagement is a fraction of that, and carries no severance risk if your needs change.

**When should I hire fractional instead of full-time?**
When you need executive judgment more than executive hours. If you need forty hours a week of
hands-on architecture, hire full-time — and I will happily help you find and evaluate that person.

**Do you work remotely?**
Yes. I am based in San Antonio, Texas and work with teams across the United States and Mexico,
travelling on-site for planning sessions and key milestones.
